#### I have a bad habit, formed over countless years, of immediately looking up whatever concerns me. "What is the best sauce to get with a Subway sandwich?" is a personal highlight that according to my search history, I've recycled multiple times.

The problem extends from Subway sauce inquiries to my studies and soul-searching. It is unbearable to come up with a question and not immediately look up an answer.  

My problem is an instinct gratification problem. It stems from how fast, effective and rewarding digital tools have become. The issue has a hundred different faces. Not everyone is addicted to internet searches; some people are addicted to talking to LLMs, others have long been addicted to social media.  

For me, if I don’t get to search up my no-doubt urgent questions, I get anxious. It’s mild anxiety, but it is present. I get restless and doubt my own reasoning skills.  

On the surface, addiction to fast information may seem harmless compared to other vices like a social media addiction (or chocolate). However, it has fundamental consequences.  

Because I’m in such a haste to rely on outside information, I don’t sit in silence, ponder and problem-solve on my own. I also start doubting my own knowledge, because the voice at the back of my head nags that it’d be better to check the internet anyway.  

Constantly looking up information also makes me consume more content than my brain can handle. I get overstimulated and confused, and all those valuable online explanations together form a vague, brown mush that is impossible to internalize.  

The solution is, of course, simple: I need to stop looking things up all the time. More broadly put, those of us struggling with information overload have to learn to starve ourselves of it. It is a form of reassurance seeking and it is interfering with our thinking.  

Us humans need time and space to process our thoughts. Private pondering is crucial in order to get better at problem-solving, come up with creative ideas and evolve one’s ever-changing identity. As anyone who’s ever learned a difficult subject would agree, boredom, frustration and uncertainty are landmarks on the road to success, and they are impossible to eliminate.  

We need to sit in silence, alone, and get bored. We need to think.  

For example: What is the best sauce to get with a Subway sandwich? For me, the answer is obviously none. I don’t even know why I keep searching it up; I like my sandwiches bare and sauceless! But if I had to get some sauce, I would have any Subway version of mustard or mayonnaise. And that’s it.  

See?  

That was a simple example, and you can do much better than me. For one, what do you want to do in life? Avoid searching up what you should do in the future or what others are doing, just sit with the question. What do you want to do? Get deep in thought and tolerate the vague uncertainty.  

It is good to think about things. And it is good to think about them all alone in silence, sometimes.
