#### I have a bad habit, formed over countless years, of immediately looking up whatever concerns me. "What is the best sauce to get with a Subway sandwich?" is a highlight of mine that according to my search history, I have recycled multiple times. 

The problem extends however from Subway sauce inquiries to my studies and even soul-searching. It is near-unbearable to come up with a question and not immediately look up a precise internet answer.  

My problem is an instant gratification problem, one tied to how fast, effective and rewarding digital tools have become. The issue can emerge in a thuosand different ways; some have become addicted to LLMs with their quick, warm replies, others to checking social media.  

The root is the same as with all addictions: For me, if I don't immediately get to search up my no-doubt mind-burning questions (for example: "Is mind-burning a real English word?", which it is not), I get anxious. It's a mild anxiety, internet searches come short of being amphetamine to me, but it is present.  

Yes, the anxiety matches all those other viceful addictions like an addiction to chocolate, but also as with all addictions, it has specific consequences.  

For me, it means I don't sit in silence, ponder and problem-solve on my own. I've developed a lack of trust in my own knowledge and memories, because at the back of my mind that nagging voice always informs me it would be better to check the internet anyway. 

Constantly looking up information makes me consume much more information than my brain can possibly handle, and so it all becomes a formless, brown mush that floats around the periphery of my mind all day and jumbles up what remaining brain activity I had.  

As I said. Consequences.  

It is one thing to notice it happening, another thing to do something about it. The solution to this issue is so simple that there is no need to look it up (—or is there?—): I should stop looking things up. More broadely put, to be inclusive of everyone with the same flavor of vice I have, we have to starve ourselves of that instant information and instant reply.  

Humans need time and space to think in order to process their thoughts, come up with new ideas, and keep forming their identity. We mustn't let all this noise hold us back from being human. We musn't let a good tool become a fatal weapon.  

We must think! *Think!* 

What is the best sauce to get with a Subway sandwich? Hmm, it depends firstly on which Subway you are at, especially if they sell or are out of different sauces. Then it depends on whether you want something sweet and salty. Mayoinnaise is a classic; it'll be there with a 99% chance. Mustard too. You have to look out for the names. Honey mustard sounds sweet. Onion sauce sounds onion-y.  

You get me?  

That was a bland example, and you can do much better than me. For one, what is your meaning in life? Don't search up what you should do in the future or what others are doing, just think about it for a moment. What do you like to do? Get deep into those thoughts and tolerate the uncertainty.

It is good to think about things. And it is good to think about them alone in silence, sometimes.
